The challenge

AfterQuery builds environments for training and testing AI agents. Their challenge: build a world where today's best agents fail, show that the failures come from real decisions rather than tricks, and keep the reward safe from judges who spend fifteen minutes trying to hack it.

How it works

  • A platform on fire: about 12 services (an e-shop, a SaaS product or a media site) that are already failing when the agent arrives.
  • 20 MCP tools to investigate and fix things, and every call moves a simulated clock forward, as in a real outage.
  • Hard, but fair: silent harm behind green dashboards, gray failures, several causes at once, misleading signals, and logs where the useful lines hide among 97% noise.
  • Reward from ground truth: traffic actually served, faults actually fixed and a correct diagnosis, minus collateral damage, data loss and cost.
  • Reproducible: deterministic and seeded, and replaying all 216 recorded runs on a fresh simulator gives every score bit for bit.

Results

Seven frontier models, 24 scenarios, 3 runs each. Every model scored below the reference run in all 21 runs.

  • Reference run: 0.969
  • Best model (gemini-4-carbon): 0.871. Kimi K3.1 Pro 0.759, DeepSeek 4.1 Flash 0.724, Nemotron 3.5 Super 0.636
  • Doing nothing, stopping early and 11 exploit policies: 0.00 (best exploit 0.028)

The hardest scenarios come from real incidents. In a Facebook-2010-style cache loop, models revert the bad config but forget to shed load first, so the platform stays down.
