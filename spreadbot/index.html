Spreadbot

A bot that runs a car-transport company for one simulated week, live, against every other team. My challenge at Junction 2026, set by Bahn Express.

The problem

Bahn moves cars for car dealers, and a freelance driver drives each car on its own wheels. Spread is that work as a simulated week in Finland. A dealer asks us for a price and asks a hidden competitor too: quote too high and we lose the car. Each car we win is posted as a gig in the drivers' app, at a fee we choose: pay too little and no driver takes it in time.

The score is the margin, what dealers pay minus what drivers are paid, and at least 90% of cars must arrive on time. Every team plays the same week, live, on one leaderboard.

How it decides

  • Quote: the price that maximises the chance of winning times the margin left after the fee and the late risk. The competitor's level is learned live, per day, dealer and lane.
  • Late risk: gradient-boosted trees trained on 804,000 past orders predict whether a car will be late (held-out AUC 0.90), and the risk is priced into every quote.
  • One fee per gig: read off a measured booking curve, anchored on the drive time, because drivers price the drive, not the list fee.
  • Gigs that end near home: two cars are paired into one gig when it ends close to where drivers live; those get booked far more often.
  • Guards for the bad week: no long drives after warnings, and a late barrier that makes an expected late car more expensive as the week's on-time share drops.

How I worked

I ran several Claude Code sessions in parallel, each on one idea, with handoffs written by Gemini and Gemma. Every idea sat behind a switch, so it could be measured with and without on the same simulated weeks, including weeks it was never tuned on. An idea was merged only if it won.

  • Pricing the late risk: +€2,076 per week on 12 unseen weeks.
  • Gigs that end near home: +€400 to €600 per week on two unseen sets.
  • One fee per gig from the booking curve: +€383 and +€541 per week on two unseen sets.
  • The guards cost about €300 per week, but all 72 unseen weeks stayed above 90% on time.
  • What lost: cheaper prices to win more cars, a looser late barrier, direct offers to drivers, three- and four-car gigs.

The final

We were 6th going into the last game-day. Then the other teams booked €1,500 to €2,000 each on their last-day cars. Our bot had a guard that holds those cars back to protect the on-time share in risky weeks, so it had little left to book.

We finished 16th with €18,423, our best live week, at 96.7% on time with every car delivered. Places 4 to 16 were within €1,550; we were €661 short of the top 10, who went on to the jury. A safety margin is a bet: in risky weeks it saved us, and in this one it cost us the jury.
