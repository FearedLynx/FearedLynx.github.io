The problem

Peatlands cover about a third of Finland, and more than half of them have been drained. Finland and the EU are now restoring them, and the EU Nature Restoration Regulation requires countries to monitor and report the progress. Today that still means experts listening a few mornings a year, so most restored sites are never checked.

How it works

  • AI on the device: BirdNET runs on the UNO Q itself, on 3-second windows, in about half a second each. No cloud, no internet.
  • Birds that mean a healthy bog: golden plover, wood sandpiper, snipe, curlew, whimbrel, crane and others, a list reviewed by Pieter van Eijk at Wetlands International.
  • A health score from 0 to 100: species heard against what a healthy bog should sound like that season. The device can learn its own site's baseline, so it works as a before-and-after tool for rewetting.
  • Both brains of the UNO Q: Linux runs the audio, the AI and a local dashboard; the microcontroller packs an hourly 16-byte LoRa-ready summary and runs a watchdog, so a remote office can tell a dead device from a quiet bog.
  • Private by design: no audio is ever stored or sent, only species names and timestamps.
