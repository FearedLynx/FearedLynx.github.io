Make-a-Ring

A ring design studio for people who are not jewellers. You scroll patterns, draw your own, see the ring on a hand at your real size, and leave with a file a 3D printer or a casting house can use.

The idea

Custom rings are designed in CAD programs made for professionals, or picked from a short list of presets. I wanted someone with an idea for a ring to be able to try it, see it at their real size and hand a maker a file that works, without learning CAD.

The idea was mine, and so was the first working version: the cross-shaped pattern carousel around a live 3D ring, the templates, the try-on and the file export. I built it on my own before the team work started.

What it does

  • Scroll to style: up and down change the middle pattern, left and right the sides, and the ring updates as you go. The arrow keys do the same.
  • Draw your own: draw one tile with mirror or kaleidoscope symmetry and it repeats seamlessly around the band.
  • Size: US, EU and UK sizes with the real inner diameter, a drawing of the ring you can resize by dragging, and a size finder that measures against a bank card.
  • Try it on: the ring at true scale on a hand, in five skin tones, or on a photo of your own hand.
  • Check and download: printability checks with each material's real limits (wall thickness, detail size, seamless wrap) and one-click fixes, an itemised cost estimate, and STL or OBJ files generated in the browser.

What I developed

Besides the idea and the first version, I also worked on the design and the interaction:

  • The studio layout: side panels with a strip of category icons, resizable and collapsible, and an interface that scales down on small or zoomed screens so the ring keeps its room.
  • The pattern editor: symmetry that also applies when erasing and moving, so a mirrored shape stays mirrored.
  • Saved designs and engraving: a library of your own rings, and inside engravings with five fonts and an adjustable depth.
  • The size step: the dimensioned ring drawing with draggable arrows.
  • Motion and sound: a curtain that sweeps across between pages, and quiet sounds such as a measuring-tape click for sizes and a chime when the files are ready, with a mute switch.
  • The brand rules: an 8-page guide for type, colour, controls, motion and sound, and a pass over the whole site to make it follow them.

Javier Arevalo joined for the hackathon. He moved the 3D ring building into a background thread so the page stays smooth, built the 3D try-on hand and the phone layout, and handled the deployment. We reviewed and merged each other's work.
